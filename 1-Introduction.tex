\section{Introduction}
\label{Sec:Introduction}

Termination and nontermination of infinite-state systems are fundamental problems in formal methods. 
Termination analysis is undecidable~\cite{DBLP:journals/x/Turing37}, 
yet there exist many techniques that succeed on a large class of real-world problems.
Modern techniques treat termination and nontermination as separate 
problems, since each has a different proof procedure.
The existence of a ranking function by e.g.~\cite{DBLP:conf/vmcai/PodelskiR04,DBLP:conf/cav/KuraUH20,DBLP:journals/jacm/Ben-AmramG14,DBLP:conf/cav/HeizmannHP14,DBLP:journals/jar/GieslABEFFHOPSS17,DBLP:conf/esop/SaritaSGSV26,DBLP:conf/sas/Ben-AmramDG19,DBLP:conf/tacas/UrbanGK16,FedyukovichZG18,RileyF25} 
witnesses termination by providing a measure that strictly decreases along every transition.
Alternatively, fixpoint computation, e.g.~\cite{DBLP:journals/pacmpl/UnnoTGK23}, and disjunctively well-founded 
transition invariants~\cite{Transition-Invariants-Revisited-CAV26,DBLP:conf/pldi/CookPR06,DBLP:conf/cav/KroeningSTW10,DBLP:conf/tacas/TsitovichSWK11} have been proposed for termination analysis.
Nontermination techniques are predominantly based on the detection of recurrent 
sets — sets of states in which every state has a successor that also belongs to the set~\cite{DBLP:conf/sas/Ben-AmramDG19,DBLP:conf/cade/FrohnG22,DBLP:conf/tacas/BrockschmidtCIK16,DBLP:conf/foveoos/BrockschmidtSOG11,DBLP:conf/cav/LarrazNORR14,FedyukovichZG18}.
There also exist alternative approaches based on Hoare-style reasoning~\cite{DBLP:conf/pldi/LeQC15}, 
safety proving~\cite{DBLP:conf/tacas/ChenCFNO14,DBLP:journals/entcs/BiereAS02,DBLP:journals/entcs/SchuppanB06}, representing infinite 
runs as sums of geometric series~\cite{DBLP:conf/tacas/LeikeH18},
or synthesis of \emph{negative} exact loop bounds~\cite{RileyF25}.


In this work, we present a unified framework for proving both termination and nontermination.
This framework extends the safety-based nontermination 
analysis~\cite{DBLP:conf/tacas/ChenCFNO14} (SNA), enabling it to prove termination.
The extension is a novel interpolation-based method for generating
disjunctively well-founded transition invariants for termination analysis.
The proposed approach overapproximates terminating transition traces constructed by SNA with
a Craig interpolation~\cite{Craig_1957}.
The interpolant is processed into a transition invariant candidate, which is used to prove termination.
This termination approach is executed in synergy with SNA.
Transition invariant candidate produced by the termination analysis allows to create smaller, 
focused (non)termination problems, which guide the analysis of the whole transition system.
Solving these problems helps to extend the transition invariant candidate and
enables an efficient nontermination analysis of complex problems.

We implemented our new technique in the \textsc{Golem} Constrained Horn Clause solver~\cite{DBLP:conf/cav/BlichaBS23}. 
We evaluated it on the Integer Transition Systems benchmarks from the Termination Competition~\cite{DBLP:conf/cade/GieslMRTW15}. 
Our tool both solved a large number of terminating instances and demonstrated significant improvement for 
nontermination analysis, compared to the SNA.
The comparison with \textsc{KoAT}~\cite{DBLP:journals/toplas/BrockschmidtEFFG16}, \textsc{LoAT}~\cite{DBLP:conf/cade/FrohnG22}, and \textsc{T2}~\cite{DBLP:conf/tacas/BrockschmidtCIK16},
participants of the Termination Competition~2025\footnote{\url{https://termination-portal.org/wiki/Termination_Competition_2025}.},
shows that our approach is particularly strong at proving nontermination and solves unique instances, including two
that were not solved by any participant of the Termination Competition~2025.

Overall, the contributions of this paper are as follows: (1) a novel interpolation-based method for the generation of 
disjunctively well-founded transition invariants, (2) a unified (non)termination analysis procedure that utilises 
the intermediate results for self-guidance and (3) its 
implementation and successful evaluation.
