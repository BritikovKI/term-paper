\subsection{Implementation}


We implemented the prototypes of Algorithms~\ref{Alg:SNA}, \ref{Alg:SNA_Term}, and \ref{Alg:SNA_Refined} in \golem;
we refer to these implementations as \sna, \snaTerm, and \snaOpt.
\golem was chosen because it supports multiple different safety verification procedures,
such as Property-Directed Reachability~\cite{DBLP:journals/fac/BradleyM08} (PDR), Transition Power Abstraction~\cite{DBLP:conf/tacas/BlichaFHS22} (TPA),
Property-Directed k-Induction~\cite{DBLP:conf/fmcad/JovanovicD16} (PDKIND), and Lazy Abstraction With Interpolants~\cite{DBLP:conf/cav/McMillan06} (LAWI).
Additionally, we used quantifier elimination, already implemented in \golem.
% \snaOpt with different safety verification engines was able to solve different
% termination problems uniquely.
% Quantifier Elimination is produced by executing Model Based Projections, untill the whole formula is covere
%
For SMT queries and interpolation, our implementation uses the \opensmt  solver~\cite{DBLP:conf/sat/HyvarinenMAS16,DBLP:conf/tacas/BruttomessoPST10}.
% \opensmt is integrated within \golem and supports interpolation.


\subsection{Evaluation}


We compared \snaOpt against \sna and \snaTerm to evaluate the effect of combining termination and nontermination analyses.
Additionally, we evaluated \snaOpt against several state-of-the-art tools:
\LoAT~\cite{DBLP:conf/cade/FrohnG22,10.1007/978-3-031-90660-2_13} (version eacb74b), \KoAT~\cite{DBLP:journals/toplas/BrockschmidtEFFG16,10.1007/978-3-031-90660-2_13}
(version 41cb681), and \Tt~\cite{DBLP:conf/tacas/BrockschmidtCIK16} (version 10f1373).
The evaluation focused on termination analysis of Integer Transition Systems.
Experiments were conducted on an Ubuntu 20.04 machine with an AMD Epyc 7742 64-core CPU and 8x32 GiB RAM.
Each benchmark was executed on a single core with 2 GiB of RAM and a wall-clock timeout of 120 seconds.
Our evaluation addressed the following research questions:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{RQ1:} How does extending \sna with the termination procedure affect verification performance?
\item \textbf{RQ2:} How does \snaOpt, alone and as a portfolio of safety engines, compare to state-of-the-art tools?
\end{itemize}

The benchmark suite of Integer Transition Systems was taken from the Termination Competition~\cite{DBLP:conf/cade/GieslMRTW15}.
Overall, there are 1222 benchmarks (538 nonterminating, 665 terminating, and 19 of unknown status).
We excluded 42 trivially terminating benchmarks, whose termination can be detected by simple syntactic checks;
all reported numbers refer to the remaining 1180 benchmarks.

\paragraph{RQ1:} Table~\ref{tab:internal} gives the experimental results for \snaOpt, \sna, and \snaTerm with TPA as the safety verification engine.
\snaOpt solves more terminating and more nonterminating instances than both \sna and \snaTerm,
including 284 instances solved by neither of them;
conversely, \sna and \snaTerm each solve 7 instances not solved by \snaOpt.
We attribute the improvement mainly to \snaOpt's focused sub-problems, which concentrate the analysis on reachable states
not covered by the transition invariant; this helps recurrent set detection and extends the transition invariant with new disjuncts.
In answer to \textbf{RQ1}, the combination of termination and nontermination reasoning in
\snaOpt solves substantially more instances than either \sna or \snaTerm.

The safety engine matters: \snaOpt solves 805 instances with TPA (34 of them not with PDKIND)
and 800 with PDKIND (29 not with TPA). Unless stated otherwise, \snaOpt uses TPA.

\paragraph{RQ2:} Termination tools typically combine several techniques,
and the safety engines of \golem are complementary.
We therefore also evaluate \emph{\snaOpt portfolio}, which runs \snaOpt with four engines
(TPA, PDKIND, PDR, and LAWI) concurrently on the same single core.
Figure~\ref{fig:stateOfArtComp} compares runtimes (orange squares: nonterminating, blue crosses: terminating;
above the diagonal: \snaOpt is faster), and Table~\ref{tab:external} the solved instances.
\snaOpt portfolio solves 827 instances (\snaOpt: 805), against 1002 for \Tt, 562 for \KoAT, and 525 for \LoAT;
as its engines share one core, this is fewer than the 834 solved by TPA and PDKIND together in separate runs.
On nontermination, \snaOpt portfolio (460) is second only to \LoAT (525) among the compared tools, ahead of \Tt (430);
on termination (367), it is behind \Tt (572) and \KoAT (562).
No answer of \snaOpt portfolio contradicts an answer of \LoAT, \KoAT, or \Tt.
\snaOpt portfolio solves nine instances that none of \LoAT, \KoAT, and \Tt solves (eight nonterminating, one terminating);
\snaOpt alone solves eight such instances.
Two of them, \texttt{juLinkedListCreateAddAll} and \texttt{juLinkedListCreateAddAllAt},
were not solved by any participant of the Termination Competition 2025; \snaOpt found recurrent sets and proved their nontermination.
In answer to \textbf{RQ2}, \snaOpt is second strongest on nontermination among the compared tools,
behind \Tt and \KoAT on termination, and complementary to all of them.
