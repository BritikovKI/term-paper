\subsection{Implementation}


We implemented the prototypes of Algorithms~\ref{Alg:SNA}, \ref{Alg:SNA_Term}, and \ref{Alg:SNA_Refined} in \golem; 
we refer to these implementations as \sna, \snaTerm, and \snaOpt.
\golem was chosen because it supports multiple different safety verification procedures,
such as Property-Directed Reachability~\cite{DBLP:journals/fac/BradleyM08} (PDR), Transition Power Abstraction~\cite{DBLP:conf/tacas/BlichaFHS22} (TPA), and Property-Directed k-Induction~\cite{DBLP:conf/fmcad/JovanovicD16} (PDKIND).
Additionally, we used quantifier elimination, already implemented in \golem.
% \snaOpt with different safety verification engines was able to solve different 
% termination problems uniquely.
% Quantifier Elimination is produced by executing Model Based Projections, untill the whole formula is covere
%
For SMT queries and interpolation, our implementation uses the \opensmt  solver~\cite{DBLP:conf/sat/HyvarinenMAS16,DBLP:conf/tacas/BruttomessoPST10}.
\opensmt is integrated within \golem and supports interpolation.


\subsection{Evaluation}


We compared \snaOpt against \sna and \snaTerm to evaluate the effect of combining termination and nontermination analyses.
Additionally, we evaluated \snaOpt against several state-of-the-art tools: 
\LoAT~\cite{10.1007/978-3-031-90660-2_13} (version eacb74b), \KoAT~\cite{10.1007/978-3-031-90660-2_13} 
(version 41cb681), \Tt~\cite{DBLP:conf/tacas/BrockschmidtCIK16} (version 10f1373). 
The evaluation focused on termination analysis of Integer Transition Systems. 
Experiments were conducted on an Ubuntu 20.04 machine with an AMD Epyc 7742 64-core CPU and 8x32 GiB RAM.
Each benchmark was executed on 2 cores with 2 GiB of allocated RAM. 
Our evaluation addressed the following research questions:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{RQ1:} How does extending \sna with the termination procedure affect verification performance?
\item \textbf{RQ2:} How does \snaOpt, as part of the \golem portfolio, compare to state-of-the-art tools?
\end{itemize}

The benchmark suite of Integer Transition Systems was taken from the Termination Competition~\cite{DBLP:conf/cade/GieslMRTW15}. 
Overall, there are 1222 benchmarks (538 nonterminating, 665 terminating, and 19 unknown). 
We excluded 42 trivially terminating benchmarks, whose termination can be detected by simple syntactic checks;
all reported numbers refer to the remaining 1180 benchmarks.
Each run had a timeout of 120 seconds.

\paragraph{RQ1:} Table~\ref{tab:internal} gives the experimental results for \snaOpt, \sna, and \snaTerm with TPA as safety solver. 
\snaOpt outperforms both \sna and \snaTerm on terminating 
and on nonterminating instances, and solves 284 instances solved by neither \sna nor \snaTerm; 
conversely, \sna and \snaTerm each solve 7 instances not solved by \snaOpt.
We attribute the improvement mainly to \snaOpt's generation of focused sub-problems, 
which allows the analysis to concentrate on specific reachable states.
% This benefits recurrent set detection by focusing the analysis on specific states not covered by the transition invariant.
% It also benefits termination proving by extending the transition invariant with additional disjuncts 
that cover different regions of the reachable state space.
In answer to \textbf{RQ1}, the combination of termination and nontermination reasoning in 
\snaOpt solves substantially more instances than either \sna or \snaTerm.

The choice of the underlying safety verification engine matters:
with TPA, \snaOpt solves 805 instances (34 uniquely), and with PDKIND, 800 (29 uniquely).

\paragraph{RQ2:} Termination tools typically combine several techniques in a portfolio, 
so we also evaluate \snaOpt within \golem, whose previous portfolio combined lasso-shaped nontermination 
detection~\cite{DBLP:journals/entcs/BiereAS02,DBLP:journals/entcs/SchuppanB06} and a counter-based termination 
argument~\cite{DBLP:conf/popl/GulwaniMC09}. We added \snaOpt with the TPA and PDKIND engines (\golem + \snaOpt).
Figure~\ref{fig:stateOfArtComp} compares runtimes (points above the diagonal: \golem + \snaOpt is faster), 
Table~\ref{tab:external} gives the solved instances.
\golem + \snaOpt solves 906 benchmarks, 238 more than the previous \golem portfolio, against 1002 for \Tt, 562 for \KoAT, and 525 for \LoAT.
On nontermination, it (501) is second only to \LoAT (525); on termination (405), it is behind \Tt (572) and \KoAT (562).
No unsound results were observed.
\golem + \snaOpt solves ten instances that no other compared tool solves.
Two benchmarks, \texttt{juLinkedListCreateAddAll} and \texttt{juLinkedListCreateAddAllAt}, 
were not solved by any participant of the Termination Competition 2025, \snaOpt proved their nontermination.
In answer to \textbf{RQ2}, \snaOpt substantially strengthens the \golem portfolio, 
making it second strongest on nontermination among the compared tools and complementary to them on termination.
